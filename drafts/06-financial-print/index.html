AWS CARRIES 62% OF LOCAL STACK, FILINGS SHOW

Five of Eight Vendors Secretly Ride a Single Landlord — 9.2 Hours of Downtime Logged on the Year; One Migration Would Sever Two of the Five Shared Paths

By The Blast Radius Desk · Compiled from the vendors’ own subprocessor filings

The eight software vendors retained by the local firm JacHammer.ai present every appearance of healthy diversification. The filings those vendors publish about themselves, however, tell a different story: five of the eight quietly subcontract their computing to a single landlord, Amazon Web Services, according to a Blast Radius review of subprocessor lists.

The Nut Graf — Read at a Glance

AWS carries 62% of your stack (5 of 8 vendors) and logged 9.2 hours of downtime last year.

Move error monitoring and one CDN path off AWS-bound vendors: a single migration severs 2 of the 5 shared AWS paths.

The concentration — 62 per cent of the company’s stack — would scarcely be remarkable were it not for the landlord’s ledger. AWS has logged 9.2 hours of downtime in the year to date, good for an exposure score of 5.75, more than four times that of the next heaviest carrier.

Executives familiar with the matter described the redundancy as “largely theatrical.” When the landlord falters, the review finds, the failure propagates backwards through the stack — taking with it AI generation, payments, monitoring and hosting in a single session.

The full accounting, engraved opposite, traces every path from the firm to its providers. Readers are invited to press upon a provider’s seal to see, in red ink, precisely how far the blast radius extends. — Continued on Page A4.

Compliance Fallout

Of particular concern to auditors: Sentry, the error-monitoring concern, routes its payloads — which may contain personally identifiable information — through Fastly, a carrier on the supply-chain watchlist holding no valid SOC 2 attestation.

Under the doctrine of inherited controls, the gap does not remain Fastly’s. It travels upstream with the payloads, and the firm’s own SOC 2 inherits the deficiency in full.

Method

Blast Radius reads the subprocessor lists each vendor is required to publish, assembles the second layer of the stack, and scores each provider by share of stack multiplied by recorded downtime. No vendor was contacted; the filings indict themselves.
